# Mechanics

## Primary-button cartridge

The current preferred direction is a bearing-supported lever rather than a flexure.

Reason:

A flexure prototype made in plastic does not transfer cleanly to a final titanium part. A bearing-defined mechanism preserves its geometry much better across prototype and final materials.

Proposed module:

```text
          titanium lever / key
──────────────────────────────────
                  │
        ○─────────┴─────────○
        bearing           bearing
                  │
                 axle
                  │
           moving magnet
                  │
              Hall sensor
                  │
        bidirectional coil
                  │
         replaceable hard stop
──────────────────────────────────
          removable cartridge
```

### Why two bearings

Two separated bearings constrain yaw and give the key a mechanically defined rotation axis.

The button only needs a small angular excursion. Example:

- finger lever arm: 45 mm;
- fingertip travel: 0.5 mm;

which corresponds to only about 0.64° of rotation.

That makes the bearing duty extremely light.

## Magnetic return

The passive magnet system should:

- hold the lever at a stable rest position;
- support normal resting finger force;
- provide return force with zero electrical power;
- avoid a fragile mechanical spring as the primary feel element.

The coil then modifies the energy/force profile transiently.

A bidirectional driver is strongly preferred because it permits both reinforcement and partial cancellation of the passive field.

Possible profiles:

- near-touch / very light;
- linear;
- progressive;
- crisp snap;
- heavy tactile;
- two-stage;
- asymmetric press/reset.

## Hall sensing

The Hall sensor is treated as a displacement sensor, not a digital switch.

Useful state:

```text
position x
velocity dx/dt
direction
resting baseline
actuation threshold
reset threshold
```

Resting-finger compensation can use a slow-moving baseline so static finger load does not become an accidental click while fast real presses remain detectable.

## Mechanical stop

The tactile click should not depend on a hard stop.

A hard stop exists only to protect the mechanism when:

- power is off;
- the user presses excessively;
- control firmware fails;
- the magnetic actuator reaches its useful travel.

Use a replaceable low-wear insert rather than metal-to-metal impact.

## Titanium button mass

The final key should be titanium but not a solid plate.

Preferred direction:

- thin CNC shell;
- ribs only where stiffness is needed;
- additional thickness near the bearing/axle interface.

Prototype dynamics can be made representative before titanium fabrication by adding ballast to a plastic/aluminium lever at the corresponding mass locations.

## Wheel

Target architecture:

```text
finger
  ↓
titanium wheel
  ↓
dual precision bearings
  ↓
magnetic absolute angle sensor
  ↓
electromagnetic torque actuator
```

The wheel should have no contact encoder.

The physical detents are virtual force fields generated by the actuator.

The same wheel can therefore support:

- true free-spin;
- 24 coarse detents;
- 48/96/etc. fine detents;
- extremely fine micro-detents;
- velocity-dependent transition to free-spin;
- software-defined landmark detents.

## Materials

Use titanium for user-facing value and long-life structure, but choose bearing/tribological materials by function.

Good candidates by role:

- shell / key / wheel: titanium;
- precision axle: hardened stainless or ceramic;
- bearings: stainless or hybrid ceramic;
- hard-stop insert: PEEK/POM/other engineering polymer;
- feet: replaceable PTFE-class material;
- ballast: tungsten.

Avoid relying on titanium-on-titanium sliding contact.

## Center of mass

The target center of mass is under the palm rather than near the optical sensor.

Design goals:

- low vertical CG;
- rear/palm bias;
- modular ballast;
- left/right trim capability if later measurements justify it.

The development chassis should permit changing ballast position before the final titanium body is committed.
