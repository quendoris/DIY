# Design Language — Keyboard

Status: **owner-approved design direction; geometry and finishes still require samples.**

## Reference interpretation

From the supplied MSI reference:
- keep high-profile, full-size/comfortable key targets;
- do not compress nav keys, inverted-T arrows or numpad into the alphanumeric block;
- retain clear separation between function row and the main key area.

From the supplied angular/cyberpunk chassis reference:
- use an articulated, clearly structural metal frame;
- combine **strict straight lines, facets and faceted beams** with subtle luminous channels;
- oval/slot-shaped luminous cutouts are allowed within the otherwise angular language, subject to stress and cleaning analysis;
- avoid the visually noisy, overbright, rainbow-heavy treatment.

## Approved direction

### Body
- Substantial metal body; titanium/aluminium variants and their mass are not decided.
- Main visual language: straight edges, crisp intersections, recessed pockets, controlled planar surfaces, purposeful angular members.
- Accent geometry may include *elongated oval apertures* or slots, not random ornamental perforation.
- The user values crisp visual boundaries, including those on every key.
- The frame must be physically comfortable: hidden/protected edges near skin, cable and hands, despite visually sharp forms.
- Strong separation between alpha area, F-row, nav cluster, arrows and numpad; exact gaps await a dimensional layout study.

### Keycaps
- Full-height keys with **visually sharp, geometrically distinct boundaries**.
- Matte or fine satin metal, not mirror chrome and not intentionally smoothed/rounded 'soap bars'.
- Titanium or appropriate machined aluminium alloy is the direction; final choice awaits feel, mass and sound trials.
- Small edge breaks/micro-chamfers are allowed *only as much as testing shows necessary* for comfort, coating/finish quality and safety.
- Key shape and edge radii are **not frozen**: evaluate side-by-side physical prototypes before final CNC.
- Completely opaque keycaps.
- **Black laser-produced legends** in a coherent bespoke visual style; no backlighting through legends.
- Test actual black marking techniques on chosen metal/finish: laser annealing/oxide coloration, dedicated black marking or fill, depending on substrate. 'Laser engraved' does not by itself guarantee permanent dark/black marks on all metals.

### Light
Lighting is intended as **architecture and interaction**, not decoration.

Layers:
1. Ultra-thin recessed contour lines reinforcing straight frame facets.
2. Soft, controlled underglow without exposed direct-view LEDs.
3. Per-key edge/seat halo through narrow diffusers/light guides around the opaque keycap.
4. Reactive mode: on press, illuminate a *local zone around that key* in red (initial concept: fast ~30-ms response followed by configurable decay). Parameters are prototypes, not final acceptance values.

Keep adjacent keys optically separated enough for localized effects. Validate diffuser uniformity, leakage into optical sensor/electronics, thermal output and visual comfort. Provide a completely-off profile.

## Packaging

There is no requirement to reproduce the exact shape of an existing keyboard. Unusual housing geometry is welcome **if key target placement and spacing remain ergonomic**.

Potential design studies:
- a clean straight-edged top plate plus articulated skeleton side rails;
- inset narrow light pipes;
- elongated oval/slot light windows along load-bearing beams;
- discrete chamfers at non-contact frame intersections;
- exposed structure beneath isolated key modules.

Mechanical apertures should be analyzed for stiffness, trapped dust/debris and accessibility, not assumed to be strength-neutral.

## Core aesthetic test

A prototype passes the design-language review if it still looks purposeful and technically precise with **every LED switched off**. Lighting should only reveal and reinforce the geometry.
